## 1. Scope and research question

The process is kept fixed to dark-matter-line emission of a neutral scalar,

\[
\chi_1+A\longrightarrow \chi_1+A+s,
\]

with the target nucleus remaining in its elastic ground state. The intermediate dark state is \(\chi_2\), and both radiation orderings on the dark-matter line are retained.

The first current set is

\[
X\in\{V,A,S\},
\]

where \(V,A,S\) label the **nucleon-side Lorentz current**, not the spin or parity of the emitted particle. The emitted particle remains the same \(0^+\) scalar \(s\).

The objective is not to add AFH response functions without weights. It is to establish a factorized expression in which the exact three-body dark-matter kernel is contracted with the nuclear response tensor generated by the selected one- and two-body current.

The central question is:

> How does the continuous \(\chi_1s\) final-state phase space reweight the elastic nuclear response to vector, axial, and scalar sources?

## 2. Literature audit and reusable results

The literature already contains the main ingredients separately. The novelty target must therefore be their consistent combination with dark-matter-line scalar radiation.

### 2.1 Radiative \(2\to3\) scattering

1. **Rundong Fang, Ji-Heng Guo, Ming Li, Jia Liu, and Xiao-Ping Wang, “High-Energy Nuclear Recoils from \(2\to3\) Dark Matter Scattering.”** This is the model basis uploaded with the project. It studies \(\chi_1N\to\chi_1N+s\), with a scalar emitted from the dark-matter line before or after vector exchange. It establishes the matched-endpoint relation \(\delta=m_s\), the extra invariant-mass continuum, and the \(D^{3/2}\) endpoint suppression.

2. **Curtin and Tsai, “The Double-Dark Portal” (2014).** This is an earlier radiative dark-matter–nucleus process with a very light emitted scalar. It establishes that the \(2\to3\) topology itself is not new, while its mass regime and interaction structure differ from the present high-threshold benchmark.

3. **Bell et al., arXiv:1905.00046.** Their photon bremsstrahlung/Migdal analysis is useful for radiation-plus-EFT methodology, but the photon is associated with the atomic/nuclear recoil process rather than the dark-matter-line scalar emission considered here.

### 2.2 General nuclear currents and responses

1. **Fitzpatrick et al., arXiv:1203.3542**, and **Anand, Fitzpatrick, and Haxton, arXiv:1308.6288**, provide the nonrelativistic operator and AFH multipole-response frameworks. The AFH response basis includes \(M,\Sigma'',\Sigma',\Phi'',\widetilde\Phi',\Delta\), with the \(M\)-\(\Phi''\) and \(\Delta\)-\(\Sigma'\) interference responses. The DMFormFactor v6.0 implementation exposes eight response channels.

2. **Hoferichter, Klos, and Schwenk, arXiv:1503.04811**, derives the chiral power counting and one-/two-body currents for distinct WIMP/nucleon Lorentz channels. A crucial lesson is that \(VV\), \(VA\), \(AV\), \(AA\), and \(SS\) are distinct channels: their two-body currents do not enter at identical orders and cannot be inferred by changing only a label on the same current.

3. **Hoferichter, Klos, Menéndez, and Schwenk, arXiv:1605.08043**, provides xenon-specific one-body and scalar two-body structure factors. In particular, it tabulates \(\mathcal F_\pi\) and \(\mathcal F_\pi^\theta\) for \(^{128,129,130,131,132,134,136}\mathrm{Xe}\). These are reusable scalar-current nuclear inputs, subject to normalization and nuclear-model matching.

4. **Menéndez, Gazit, and Schwenk, arXiv:1208.1094**, and **Klos et al., arXiv:1304.7684**, provide chiral one- and two-body axial-current calculations and structure-factor fits for \(^{129}\mathrm{Xe}\) and \(^{131}\mathrm{Xe}\), including nuclear-current uncertainty bands.

5. **Filandri and Viviani, Phys. Rev. C 110, 034002 (2024), DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevC.110.034002**, applies a chiral-EFT current framework to scalar, pseudoscalar, vector, axial, and tensor sources in \(^{2}\mathrm H\) and \(^{4}\mathrm{He}\). It is a useful independent methodological reference, although it does not provide heavy-xenon responses.

### 2.3 Novelty boundary

The following are established and should not be claimed as new by themselves:

- a \(2\to3\) radiative dark-matter scattering topology;
- the existence of vector, axial, or scalar one-/two-body nuclear currents;
- AFH multipole responses;
- chiral-EFT matching of elastic WIMP–nucleus currents.

The intended new contribution is the **three-body dark-matter response kernel for each Lorentz channel and its consistent contraction with the corresponding elastic nuclear current tensor**, followed by a reproducible xenon calculation.

## 3. Kinematics and factorization

Use separate momenta throughout:

\[
q_A^\mu=P_A'^\mu-P_A^\mu,\qquad
k_s^\mu,\qquad
q_\chi^\mu=p_\chi'^\mu-p_\chi^\mu.
\]

Momentum conservation gives

\[
p_\chi^\mu+P_A^\mu
=p_\chi'^\mu+P_A'^\mu+k_s^\mu,
\qquad
p_\chi^\mu-p_\chi'^\mu=q_A^\mu+k_s^\mu.
\]

The mediator exchanged with the nucleus carries \(q_A\). The two internal \(\chi_2\) momenta are

\[
r_{\rm before}=p_\chi-k_s,\qquad
r_{\rm after}=p_\chi'+k_s.
\]

For an initially stationary nucleus,

\[
Q_A^2=|\mathbf q_A|^2=2m_AE_R+E_R^2,\qquad
t_A=q_A^2=-2m_AE_R.
\]

The AFH variable is

\[
y_A=\left(\frac{Q_A b_A}{2}\right)^2.
\]

For the project's impulse-matching convention, the one-body nucleon current uses \(q_N^\mu=q_A^\mu\), hence \(t_N=t_A\). This convention must remain distinct from the static-nucleon prescription \(t_N=-Q_A^2\). The current project records the formal impulse convention in its P3/P4 matching audit. For two-body exchange currents, the internal pion/exchange momenta are separate variables; their vector sum, not either individual momentum, gives the external transfer.

### 3.1 Generic factorized amplitude

Let \(X\) label the nuclear source and let \(r_X\) be its Lorentz rank (\(r_V=r_A=1\), \(r_S=0\)). A general single-exchange amplitude can be written

\[
\mathcal M_X^{(3)}
=
C_X\,
\mathcal T_X^{(3)\,\alpha_X}\,
D_{X,\alpha_X\beta_X}(q_A)\,
\mathcal J_X^{A,\beta_X}(q_A),
\]

with the Lorentz indices absent for \(S\). Here:

- \(\mathcal T_X^{(3)}\) is the dark-matter-line radiation kernel;
- \(D_X\) is the mediator propagator or low-energy Wilson kernel;
- \(\mathcal J_X^A=\langle A'|J_X^A|A\rangle\) is the elastic nuclear-current matrix element;
- \(C_X\) contains the couplings and any fixed normalization.

For a scalar emission vertex \(\Gamma_s=1\), define

\[
S_2(r)=
\frac{\slashed r+m_{\chi_2}}
{r^2-m_{\chi_2}^2+i\,\mathcal I(r)},
\]

where \(\mathcal I(r)\) denotes the denominator-specific width prescription already fixed by the benchmark. For a chosen dark transition bilinear \(\Gamma_{\chi X}^{\alpha_X}\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal T_X^{(3)\,\alpha_X}
=
\bar u_{\chi_1}(p_\chi')
\left[
\Gamma_{\chi X}^{\alpha_X}S_2(p_\chi-k_s)\Gamma_s
+
\Gamma_s S_2(p_\chi'+k_s)\Gamma_{\chi X}^{\alpha_X}
\right]
u_{\chi_1}(p_\chi).
}
\]

This reproduces the two coherent radiation orderings of the original model when \(\Gamma_{\chi V}^{\mu}=\gamma^\mu\). The two terms must be added before squaring.

### 3.2 Three-body response coefficient

At fixed \(E_R\) and incident speed \(v\), integrate only over the unobserved final \(\chi_1+s\) variables:

\[
\mathcal R_{XY}^{(3)\,\alpha_X\alpha_Y}
(E_R,v)
=
\int d\Phi_{\chi s}\,
\overline{
\mathcal T_X^{(3),\alpha_X}
\mathcal T_Y^{(3),\alpha_Y*}
}.
\]

The phase-space measure includes the exact three-body constraints and the chosen spin averages. The differential rate then has the schematic form

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d\sigma}{dE_R}
=
\sum_{X,Y}
C_XC_Y^*\,
\mathcal R_{XY}^{(3)\,\alpha_X\alpha_Y}
D_{X,\alpha_X\beta_X}
D_{Y,\alpha_Y\beta_Y}^{*}
\mathcal W_{XY}^{A,\beta_X\beta_Y}.
}
\]
For scalar currents the corresponding Lorentz indices are absent. For mixed-rank channels, retain only the indices belonging to the non-scalar current.

All Lorentz contractions are understood. For rank-one currents, \(\mathcal W_{XY}^{A}\) is a nuclear tensor; for scalar currents it is a scalar response. If only one channel is present, only its diagonal term remains. If multiple interactions coexist, interference terms must be retained whenever they are allowed by the quantum numbers and share the same final state.

**Important consequence:** because the nucleus remains in the same elastic ground state, the nuclear matrix element at a fixed \(q_A\) is the same elastic nuclear-current matrix element used in a \(2\to2\) calculation. The additional scalar does not by itself require a new nuclear wave function or a new AFH basis. What changes is the dark-matter coefficient \(\mathcal R^{(3)}\), the allowed recoil/phase-space domain, and—when new one-/two-body currents are introduced—the nuclear current being contracted.

Thus the useful new object is a **three-body kinematic response coefficient**, not an unweighted sum of AFH response functions.

## 4. Lorentz channels and their nuclear matching

### 4.1 Vector nucleon current \(V\)

At the one-nucleon level,

\[
\langle N'|J_V^\mu|N\rangle
=
\bar u_N(p_N')
\left[
F_1^V(t_N)\gamma^\mu
+\frac{iF_2^V(t_N)}{2m_N}\sigma^{\mu\nu}q_{N,\nu}
\right]
u_N(p_N).
\]

This is the existing VV benchmark. The current AFH/P3/P4 work already provides the relevant vector-current projection and its \(M,\Phi'',\Delta,\Sigma'\) structures and interference terms. It is the mandatory recovery test for the general framework.

The vector current obeys the appropriate continuity relation when the nuclear Hamiltonian and current are treated consistently. The full massive-vector propagator should be retained until the current-conservation reduction is explicitly justified.

### 4.2 Axial nucleon current \(A\)

For an isovector one-nucleon current, use

\[
\langle N'|J_A^\mu|N\rangle
=
\bar u_N(p_N')
\left[
G_A(t_N)\gamma^\mu\gamma_5
+\frac{G_P(t_N)}{2m_N}q_N^\mu\gamma_5
\right]\frac{\tau^a}{2}
u_N(p_N),
\]

up to the chosen sign convention for \(q_N\) and \(G_P\). Second-class induced-tensor terms can be added if the model requires them. Isoscalar/strange axial sources require their own flavor decomposition and singlet-anomaly treatment.

The leading spatial current contains transverse and longitudinal spin components. In the AFH basis these project onto \(\Sigma'\) and \(\Sigma''\), respectively. The induced pseudoscalar term is longitudinal and is tied to PCAC; it must not be omitted at the xenon recoil momenta of interest.

In the pion-pole approximation,

\[
G_P(Q_N^2)\simeq
\frac{4m_N^2}{m_\pi^2+Q_N^2}G_A(Q_N^2),
\]

so the longitudinal one-body coefficient becomes

\[
G_A-\frac{Q_N^2}{4m_N^2}G_P
\simeq
G_A\frac{m_\pi^2}{m_\pi^2+Q_N^2}.
\]

For \(^{131}\mathrm{Xe}\), \(E_R=200\)–\(269.9\) keV corresponds approximately to \(Q_N=0.221\)–\(0.257\) GeV under the formal impulse convention. With \(m_\pi=138.04\) MeV, the pion-pole factor is approximately \(0.28\)–\(0.22\). This is a **nucleon-level longitudinal amplitude factor**, not the total nuclear axial rate. The transverse axial coefficient is not suppressed by this same factor.

The axial current is not conserved. Therefore:

- do not impose \(q_{A\mu}J_A^\mu=0\);
- retain the induced-pseudoscalar/PCAC contribution;
- do not drop the longitudinal part of a massive spin-1 propagator without a controlled argument or a specified UV completion;
- treat axial charge, spatial axial current, and two-body exchange currents consistently.

The channel label must also specify the dark-side bilinear. In particular:

| Channel | Dark transition current | Nucleon current | Dark three-body kernel |
|---|---|---|---|
| \(VV\) | \(\bar\chi_2\gamma^\mu\chi_1\) | \(J_V^\mu\) | existing vector kernel |
| \(VA\) | \(\bar\chi_2\gamma^\mu\chi_1\) | \(J_A^\mu\) | same dark vector kernel as \(VV\) |
| \(AV\) | \(\bar\chi_2\gamma^\mu\gamma_5\chi_1\) | \(J_V^\mu\) | new axial-dark kernel |
| \(AA\) | \(\bar\chi_2\gamma^\mu\gamma_5\chi_1\) | \(J_A^\mu\) | new axial-dark kernel |

Thus replacing only the nucleon vector current by an axial current gives \(VA\), not \(AA\). These channels have different chiral power counting and different three-body dark tensors.

For the nuclear side, Menéndez–Gazit–Schwenk and Klos et al. provide \(^{129,131}\mathrm{Xe}\) axial structure factors including leading long-range two-body currents and uncertainty bands. Their multipole conventions and normalizations must be mapped to AFH before their tabulated fits are used as numerical inputs. They are an excellent independent benchmark for \(\Sigma'\), \(\Sigma''\) and the axial-current uncertainty.

### 4.3 Scalar nucleon current \(S\)

The scalar source must be defined at the quark/gluon level before matching. A standard mass-weighted chiral-EFT source can be written

\[
J_S=
\sum_{q=u,d,s} C_q^S\,m_q\bar q q
+
C_g^S\,\frac{\alpha_s}{\pi}G_{\mu\nu}^aG^{a\mu\nu}.
\]

A source proportional to \(\bar q q\) without \(m_q\) is a different Wilson-coefficient convention and must not be silently identified with the mass-weighted source. Heavy-quark matching and the trace-anomaly contribution must be included according to the chosen renormalization scale and operator basis.

At one-body level, define the normalization of the scalar nucleon form factor by

\[
\langle N'|J_S|N\rangle
=
F_S^N(t_N)\,\bar u_N(p_N')u_N(p_N).
\]

For the mass-weighted source above, the dimension and normalization of \(F_S^N\) follow the Wilson-coefficient convention; state them explicitly before numerical use. The leading nuclear operator is a proton/neutron-weighted density. Its leading one-body multipole has the density-type \(M\) operator structure. This does not make its numerical response identical to vector \(W_M\): proton/neutron weights, scalar nucleon form factors, and scalar two-body terms must be matched separately.

The scalar two-body current is not, in general, a redefinition of the one-body scalar form factor. Chiral EFT produces pion-exchange and trace-anomaly-related two-body terms. Hoferichter et al. (2016) provide xenon structure factors \(\mathcal F_\pi\) and \(\mathcal F_\pi^\theta\) for seven isotopes. Their fit convention is

\[
\mathcal F_\pi(u)=e^{-u/2}\sum_{i=0}^{5}c_i^\pi u^i,\qquad
\mathcal F_\pi^\theta(u)=e^{-u/2}\sum_{i=0}^{5}c_i^\theta u^i,
\]

\[
u=\frac{Q_A^2b_A^2}{2}=2y_A.
\]

The conversion \(u=2y_A\) is essential when reusing these data alongside AFH files. For \(^{131}\mathrm{Xe}\), their Table 7 gives \(b=2.2925\) fm and

| \(i\) | \(c_i^\pi\) | \(c_i^\theta\) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | -2.47546 | -25.3895 |
| 1 | 2.08643 | 19.2032 |
| 2 | -0.602812 | -5.03573 |
| 3 | 0.0824072 | 0.658108 |
| 4 | -0.00570646 | -0.0487362 |
| 5 | 0.000165335 | 0.00167317 |

These are amplitude-level nuclear structure inputs in the conventions of that paper, not AFH \(W_M\) values. Before combining them with the project's physical-\(A^2\) AFH normalization, the amplitude normalization, isospin decomposition, Wilson coefficients, oscillator length, and shell-model assumptions must be matched. The paper finds typical scalar two-body corrections at the several-percent level in its specified elastic benchmarks; that number is not a universal prediction for the present model.

The scalar mediator/current is a distinct Lorentz-rank-zero channel. Its dark kernel is

\[
\mathcal T_S^{(3)}
=
\bar u_{\chi_1}(p_\chi')
\left[
S_2(p_\chi-k_s)+S_2(p_\chi'+k_s)
\right]
u_{\chi_1}(p_\chi)
\]

for scalar transition vertices at both the emission and scattering vertices. It is not obtained by reusing the vector \(L_{\mu\nu}^{(3)}\). The same three-body phase-space domain can be reused, but a new scalar integrand and scalar response coefficient must be calculated.

### 4.4 A terminology guard

The Lorentz-scalar nucleon source \(J_S=\bar NN\) is not the same object as the AFH \(\Phi''\) nuclear multipole. \(\Phi''\) is a spin-orbit-related nuclear multipole in the AFH decomposition. A scalar source has a leading density-type \(M\) projection, plus its own chiral two-body nuclear operators. The two meanings of “scalar” must never be conflated.

In contrast, the leading coherent vector and scalar density responses receive contributions from the even isotopes as well.

## 6. Recommended calculation sequence

### Stage 1 — lock the operator/channel definitions

1. Freeze the radiation vertex \(\Gamma_s=1\) and the intermediate-state propagator/width prescription.
2. Define \(VV,VA,AV,AA,SS\) explicitly. Do not use a single ambiguous “A” label.
3. Specify mediator propagators and UV completion where longitudinal spin-1 exchange matters.
4. Fix scalar Wilson coefficients, quark-mass convention, gluon operator, and renormalization scale.

### Stage 2 — derive and validate the dark three-body kernels

1. Recover the existing \(VV\) kernel exactly.
2. Derive \(VA\) using the same dark vector kernel and the axial nuclear current.
3. Derive \(AV\) and \(AA\) with the axial dark transition bilinear.
4. Derive \(SS\) as a scalar kernel.
5. For each kernel, compare the integrated result with a direct spinor-amplitude-squared calculation at fixed \(E_R,v\). Retain both radiation diagrams and all allowed interference.

### Stage 3 — current matching and nuclear responses

1. \(V\): reuse the validated AFH one-body projection and then add only the chiral two-body terms required by the selected \(V_\chi\otimes V_N\) channel.
2. \(A\): implement \(G_A,G_P\), the PCAC relation, axial charge and spatial multipoles, then the leading two-body axial current. Compare against the \(^{129,131}\mathrm{Xe}\) Klos/Menéndez fits.
3. \(S\): implement \(F_S^p,F_S^n\), the scalar one-body density, and the \(\mathcal F_\pi,\mathcal F_\pi^\theta\) two-body terms. Compare against the published seven-isotope xenon structure factors.

### Stage 4 — physical rates and independent checks

1. Integrate the channel-specific \(\mathcal R_{XY}^{(3)}\) over the already validated three-body phase space.
2. Contract with the properly normalized nuclear response tensors.
3. Produce fixed-speed \(d\sigma/dE_R\) and channel ratios before performing halo folding.
4. Fold the seven-isotope natural-xenon response and then apply the LZ efficiency/exposure.
5. Check zero-radiation/constant-amplitude limits, current-conservation or PCAC identities, normalization recovery, convergence, and independent direct-amplitude calculations.

Every new numerical result must record the exact source commit, input hashes, environment, hardware, integration parameters, native outputs, convergence/error analysis, and an independent physical check. No numerical result is claimed by this analytic note.

## 7. Research positioning

A defensible paper-level claim is:

> We construct and implement the three-body dark-matter response kernels for dark-matter-line scalar radiation and match them to vector, axial, and scalar nuclear currents, including the relevant elastic one- and two-body nuclear responses.

Do not claim that AFH, chiral one-/two-body currents, or radiative \(2\to3\) scattering are individually new. The paper's value must come from the exact three-body current kernels, their channel-dependent contraction with the nuclear responses, and the quantitative xenon recoil predictions.

The first controlled comparison should be:

- \(VV\): reproduce the existing project benchmark;
- \(VA\): change only the nuclear current while keeping the dark vector radiation kernel fixed;
- \(AA\): quantify the genuinely spin-dependent response with its own dark axial kernel;
- \(SS\): use a scalar mediator and scalar dark transition kernel, with both one- and two-body scalar nuclear terms.

This sequence isolates the nuclear-current change from the change in the dark-matter current and mediator structure.
